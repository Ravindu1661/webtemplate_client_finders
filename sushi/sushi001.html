Our Philosophy

Nothing is added.
Nothing is missing.

SHUN was built on a single conviction: that the finest ingredient needs the fewest hands between the water and the counter. Our rice is cooked in small batches through the evening service. Our fish arrives once, sometimes twice, a day from Toyosu — never frozen, never held longer than its peak.

There is no menu to hide behind here. Only the chef, the guest, and whatever the season has decided to give us tonight.

Sushi is not decoration on rice. It is a conversation between two ingredients that ends the moment you taste it. — Kenji Watanabe, Executive Chef
The Omakase

Six Courses, One Season

Every seating follows the chef's hand, not a printed page. What follows is a single evening, as it unfolded this week.

01 — Appetizer
蛤の茶碗蒸し

Hamaguri Chawanmushi

A silken steamed egg custard built on a broth of clam and bonito, finished with a single shaving of yuzu peel to wake the palate before the rice begins.

02 — Nigiri
江戸前握り

Edomae Nigiri Selection

Eight pieces, one at a time, each seasoned individually with soy, salt, or citrus as it's set in front of you — never the same brush twice.

03 — Sashimi
刺身盛り合わせ

Sashimi Moriawase

Five cuts chosen the same morning at Toyosu, arranged by texture rather than color, served on a single block of hand-carved ice.

04 — Special Roll
金目鯛巻き

Kinmedai Maki

Lightly seared golden-eye snapper rolled with shiso and pickled myoga, brushed with a reduction of its own bones.

05 — Chef's Signature
大トロたたき 柚子胡椒

Toro Tataki, Yuzu Kosho

Fatty tuna belly, lightly torched and hand-minced, dressed with a whisper of fermented chili-yuzu. The dish Chef Watanabe is asked for most.

06 — Dessert
抹茶葛餅

Matcha Kuzumochi

Kudzu-root jelly dusted in roasted soybean flour, served with a ceremonial-grade matcha syrup and a single candied cherry blossom.

Behind the Counter

Kenji Watanabe

Executive Chef & Owner

Chef Watanabe trained for eleven years inside three of Tsukiji's most demanding kitchens before opening SHUN's twelve-seat counter in 2006. He still selects every piece of fish himself, four mornings a week, before the market opens to the public.

1998
Apprenticeship begins under Master Ito, Tsukiji Market, Tokyo.
2003
Named Head Itamae at a three-generation Ginza sushi-ya.
2006
Opens SHUN with a single twelve-seat hinoki counter.
2019
Awarded Tokyo's Culinary Heritage Recognition for Edomae craft.
— K. Watanabe
